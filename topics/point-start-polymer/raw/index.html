The critical polymer measure

Overview · Digested · Raw

A critical planar polymer from a prescribed point

Abstract

We give a proof, using the existing critical stochastic heat flow, of convergence of the normalized Gaussian nearest-neighbour polymer from a prescribed deterministic point to a random probability on continuous paths, with free terminal endpoint. The limit is a measurable function of the stochastic heat flow. A temporal regularization converges to it in probability, and the discrete polymer converges in distribution. The proof separates cancellation of the initial random mass from control of the initial path. It includes the operator argument needed to identify paths at a fixed positive cutoff, and a finite-lattice estimate uniform over the cutoffs used near time zero. The imported stochastic heat flow, Gaussian convexity, renewal, and fourth-moment results are stated with their source hypotheses.

1 The polymer and its limit

The critical stochastic heat flow is a measure in both endpoint variables. It therefore does not give a kernel at an arbitrary prescribed point by evaluation. Our object is instead the limit of the normalized discrete polymer from that point. A short initial segment carries a random scalar mass. Cancellation of that mass controls later observables, but does not by itself control excursions near the starting time. We prove these two assertions separately.

Let \(S\) be simple symmetric nearest-neighbour walk on \(\mathbb{Z} ^2\), and set \(q_j(z)=P_0(S_j=z)\). The walk expectation \(E_z\) is distinct from the disorder expectation \(\mathbb{E} \). Let \((\omega _{j,z})\) be independent standard Gaussians. For even \(N\), choose \(\beta _N\ge 0\) so that

\begin{equation} R_N=\sum _{j=1}^Nq_{2j}(0),\qquad \nu _N=e^{\beta _N^2}-1=\frac 1{R_N}\left (1+\frac {\vartheta _N}{\log N}\right ),\qquad \vartheta _N\longrightarrow \vartheta \in \mathbb{R} \,.\tag{1.1}\end{equation}

Only sufficiently large \(N\), for which the right side is positive, are used. Put \(W_{j,z}=\exp (\beta _N\omega _{j,z}-\beta _N^2/2)\) and \(\xi _{j,z}=W_{j,z}-1\). Transfers exclude entrance-time noise and include exit-time noise:

\begin{equation} Z_{a,b}(z,w)=E_z\left [\prod _{j=a+1}^b W_{j,S_{j-a}}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{b-a}=w\}}\right ],\qquad Z_{a,a}=I\,.\tag{1.2}\end{equation}

Here the disorder labels are physical times. Thus \(Z_{a,b}Z_{b,c}=Z_{a,c}\). Rows act on the left; \(Z_{a,b}(\phi ,f)=\phi Z_{a,b}f\).

Fix \(x\in \mathbb{R} ^2\), and choose a nearest even-parity site \(x_N\) to \(\sqrt {N/2}\,x\). Let \(X_N\) interpolate \(\sqrt {2/N}\,S_j\) at time \(j/N\), with \(S_0=x_N\). On \(\Gamma =C([0,1],\mathbb{R} ^2)\), define

\begin{equation} \mathsf{Q} _N^x(F)=\frac {E_{x_N}[F(X_N)\prod _{j=1}^NW_{j,S_j}]}{Z_{0,N}(x_N,1)}\,.\tag{1.3}\end{equation}

Write \(\mathsf{M} _N^x\) for the numerator measure in (1.3). The terminal weight is \(1\): no terminal point is prescribed. All weak topologies below are those of finite measures or probability measures on the indicated Polish path space.

Write \([u]=2\lceil u/2\rceil \). For \(m=[\delta N]\), omit disorder at times \(1,\ldots ,m\) in the numerator of (1.3). Denote the resulting finite measure and its normalization by \(\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _{N,\delta }^x\) and \(\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _{N,\delta }^x\). Its mass is \(Z_{m,N}(q_m^{(x_N)},1)\), where \(q_m^{(z)}(w)=q_m(w-z)\).

Let \(\mathscr Z^\vartheta \) be the critical stochastic heat flow in the convention of [1, Theorem A], with mean \(dx\,g_{t-s}(y-x)\,dy\), where

\begin{equation*} g_t(z)=(2\pi t)^{-1}\exp (-|z|^2/(2t))\,. \end{equation*}

Let \(\mathsf{M} _{[\delta ,1]}\) be its existing all-start path measure [4, Theorems 2.10 and 2.13], equivalently [7, Proposition 2.4]. Weight it by \(g_\delta (X_\delta -x)\) and prepend a Brownian bridge from \(x\) to \(X_\delta \) of duration \(\delta \), integrated as a probability kernel. This defines \(\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x\) on \(\Gamma \). Set

\begin{equation} W_\delta ^x=\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x(1)=\mathscr Z_{\delta ,1}^\vartheta (g_\delta (\cdot -x),1),\qquad \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x=\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x/W_\delta ^x\,.\tag{1.4}\end{equation}

Section 3 proves that this normalization is finite and strictly positive. All these measures are functions of one common stochastic heat flow.

Theorem 1.1. Under (1.1), there is a stochastic-heat-flow-measurable random probability \(\mathsf{Q} ^x\) on \(\Gamma \) such that, almost surely, \(\mathsf{Q} ^x\{X(0)=x\}=1\), and

\begin{equation} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x\xrightarrow [\delta \downarrow 0]{\mathbb{P} }\mathsf{Q} ^x, \qquad \mathsf{Q} _N^x\xRightarrow [N\to \infty ]{\operatorname{Law} }\mathsf{Q} ^x\,.\tag{1.5}\end{equation}
Both limits hold in the weak topology, jointly for any prescribed finite family of deterministic starting points, with the same environment and the same limiting stochastic heat flow for that family.

The first convergence is on the continuum probability space. The second is convergence in distribution over the discrete environment; no coupling of all microscopic environments is asserted. The proof uses the exact literature results specified in Section 2. It does not prescribe a terminal point, construct an uncountable spatially regular family, establish non-Gaussian universality, or identify arbitrary spatial shrinking-ball regularizations.

1.1 Relation to existing polymer measures

Clark and Mian construct an infinite, unnormalized all-start measure on continuous paths [4, Theorem 2.13]. Its finite endpoint-weighted restrictions can be normalized when their mass is finite and positive. Clark and Tsai establish the required positivity and explicitly define such probabilities [5, Corollary 1.3 and (1.8)]. Thus it would be incorrect to describe all of those constructions as lacking normalized polymer probabilities. A deterministic positive starting density and a point mass at a prescribed point are different initial conditions. Disintegration of an all-start random measure specifies conditional laws only almost everywhere for its random starting marginal, and does not select a law at a prescribed deterministic \(x\).

In the subcritical intermediate-disorder regime, Gabriel proves convergence of the normalized deterministic-start, free-endpoint polymer probability to Brownian motion [6, Theorem 1.2]. His strength is \(\beta _N=\widehat \beta /\sqrt {R_N}\) with \(0<\widehat \beta <1\), rather than (1.1). His spatial scale \(S/\sqrt N\) yields \(B/\sqrt 2\); our scale yields standard Brownian motion. The present theorem concerns the critical window and a random limiting probability.

The initial scalar cancellation is already present in [1, Proposition 4.4 and Section 7.5]. The additional work needed here is control of arbitrary deterministic tail path observables, identification of the fixed-cutoff path law, and a cutoff-uniform estimate for the original discrete prefix. We make no priority claim for these deductions. The conditional Gaussian multiplicative chaos relation in [5, Theorem 1.1] concerns the existing all-start measures; no extension of that relation to \(\mathsf{Q} ^x\) is claimed.

1.2 Coordinates and parameters

Let \(C^\vartheta \) denote the original flow of [2] and \(D(z)=\sqrt 2z\). Changing both planar endpoint coordinates gives

\begin{equation} \mathscr Z_{s,t}^\vartheta =4(D\times D)_\#C_{s,t}^\vartheta \ \stackrel {\operatorname{Law} }=\ 2C_{2s,2t}^{\vartheta -\log 2}\,.\tag{1.6}\end{equation}

The second identity is [2, (1.12)]; the first follows directly from the \(N/4\) kernel density in the two coordinate conventions. The published introduction of [4] uses \(2C_{2s,2t}^{\eta +\gamma _{\rm E}-\log 2}\). Hence its parameter, and Tsai’s parameter, are

\begin{equation} \eta _{\rm CM}=\theta _{\rm Tsai}=\vartheta -\gamma _{\rm E}\,;\tag{1.7}\end{equation}

see also [1, Remark 1.1]. We use the marked-product construction of the deterministically transformed common flow in (1.6). An equality in law is not used to couple different cutoffs independently. The parity cells have area \(v_N=4/N\), and the limiting Brownian generator is \(\Delta /2\).

2 Inputs and finite-lattice estimates

We state the imported estimates before using them. The point-start path limit is not among the inputs. The shape and buffer estimates proved in Appendix A give a common finite- \(N\) range; an estimate valid eventually for each fixed cutoff alone would not suffice near time zero.

Gaussian inverse moments. For a finite mean-one partition mass \(U\) with deterministic probability starting row and flat terminal weight, Gaussian convexity gives

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} U^{-p}\le (\mathbb{E} U^2)^{p(p+1)/2}\qquad (p\ge 0)\,.\tag{2.1}\end{equation}

This is [1, Lemma 3.2, (3.5)], applied to the finite convex log-sum-exp \(\log U\). Its positive exponential moments are finite. It also holds conditionally on the physical past, with a past-measurable probability row held fixed and the independent future noise averaged. It is never applied under a future-dependent event as a new noise law.

Critical renewal. Put \(M_N(h)=\mathbb{E} Z_{0,h}(0,1)^2\) and \(\kappa _N(h)=\nu _N M_N(h)\). Uniformly for \(\vartheta _N\) in a fixed compact interval \(I\) and \(0\le h\le TN\),

\begin{equation} \kappa _N(h)\le \frac {C_{I,T}}{\log (1+N/h)}\quad (h\ge 1),\qquad \sup _{h\le TN}\kappa _N(h)\le C_{I,T}\,.\tag{2.2}\end{equation}

We use [1, (4.1), Claim B.1], not its Proposition 4.1. If \(\Theta _N(h)=\pi /\nu _N-\pi R_h>0\), the same claim gives

\begin{equation} 0\le \pi /\Theta _N(h)-\kappa _N(h)\le C\Theta _N(h)^{-2}\,.\tag{2.3}\end{equation}

In particular, choosing a sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon >0\) makes \(\Theta _N([\epsilon N])\) bounded above and below by positive constants, with the lower constant large enough to absorb the error. Monotonicity of \(M_N\) then yields

\begin{equation} \inf _{N/4\le h\le N}\kappa _N(h)\ge c_I>0\,.\tag{2.4}\end{equation}

The upper estimate is uniform on \(I\) by monotonicity in \(\nu _N\); the scalar error estimate is uniform there. We also use the Gaussian walk bounds, local limit theorem, and \(R_h=\pi ^{-1}\log h+c+o(1)\).

Fourth moments. Theorem 1.11 and (1.28)–(1.33) of [3], in version 2, state the following specialization. Fix \(\widehat t,c_1,c_2,c_3>0\). There are \(L_4,C_4\) such that, for any \(\beta \ge 0\), duration \(h\ge L_4\), and deterministic probability \(\phi \), if

\begin{align} &0<D(\phi ):=\sum _z|z-m_\phi |^2\phi (z)<\infty ,\qquad \left\|\phi \right\|_2^2\le c_2/D(\phi ),\nonumber \\ &\sum _z\phi (z)e^{2\widehat t|z-z_0|/\sqrt h}\le c_1\ \text { for some }z_0, \qquad \operatorname{Var} Z_h^\beta (\phi ,1)\le c_3\,,\tag{2.5}\end{align}

then \(\mathbb{E} |Z_h^\beta (\phi ,1)-1|^4\le C_4(\operatorname{Var} Z_h^\beta (\phi ,1))^2\). Here \(m_\phi =\sum _z z\phi (z)\). The end-included convention is [3, (1.17)–(1.18)]. No retuning from \(\beta _N\) to a duration-dependent temperature is made. Appendix A verifies these hypotheses for long heat-started blocks and proves the short and point-started blocks directly.

The stochastic heat flow and its path measure. We retain [2, (1.11), Theorems 1.1 and 9.1, (3.51)–(3.56)]: joint endpoint-flow convergence and its mixed second-moment limit. In the coordinates above the kernel density is \((N/4)Z_{[Ns],[Nt]}(x_N,y_N)\). The critical condition in [2, (1.11)] is exactly (1.1), since for Gaussian disorder \(e^{\Lambda (2\beta )-2\Lambda (\beta )}-1=e^{\beta ^2}-1\). Thus \(\vartheta _N\to \vartheta \) is allowed. Finite lists of intervals, including overlapping intervals, converge to one flow. The limit has independent increments on disjoint time intervals and the heat-kernel mean. We retain the full self-adjoint, strongly continuous two-replica moment semigroup \(\mathcal Q_2(t)\) at (1.7) from [7, (1.2), (2.13), (2.16) and the following text]. The centered formula (2.18) there is not this full operator.

We use the continuous-path measure, its Wiener first moment and its measurable marked composition from [4, Theorems 2.10, 2.13 and Proposition 2.14] and [7, Definition 1.1 and Proposition 2.4]. The latter permits all \(L^2\)-applicable approximate identities and makes the marked-product limit independent of that choice. This fact is needed to compare compactly supported connectors with the heat connectors in [4]. Section 3 proves the necessary discrete path identification from these inputs; endpoint convergence is not itself a path-convergence theorem.

2.1 The finite-buffer output

Fix \(0<\alpha <1\) and \(\rho _0>24\). Appendix A constructs the signed heat-relative norm \(\left\|\cdot \right\|_{n,-\alpha }\) and proves the following consequence. There are constants \(C,N_*,J_0,L_0\), depending only on \(I,\alpha ,\rho _0\), with

\begin{equation} L_0\ge \max (2J_0\log \rho _0,2\log 8,4\log 3,e),\qquad L_0\le L=\log (1/\delta )\le \log N\,,\tag{2.6}\end{equation}

for which the estimates below hold for every even \(N\ge \max (N_*,16)\) and every starting site of the chosen parity. Put \(m=[\delta N]\), \(n=[\sqrt \delta N]\), and

\begin{equation} \mu _n=\frac {Z_{0,n}(x_N,\cdot )}{Z_{0,n}(x_N,1)},\qquad \widehat \mu _n=\frac {q_m^{(x_N)}Z_{m,n}}{q_m^{(x_N)}Z_{m,n}1},\qquad \eta =\mu _n-\widehat \mu _n\,.\tag{2.7}\end{equation}

Let \(\mathscr F_n=\sigma (\omega _{j,z}:j\le n)\) and define the full-horizon response of a zero-mass row by

\begin{equation} H(\eta )^2=\sum _{r=1}^{N-n}\kappa _N(N-n-r)\left\|\eta q_r\right\|_2^2 =\mathbb{E} [|\eta Z_{n,N}1|^2\mid \mathscr F_n]\,.\tag{2.8}\end{equation}

There is a concrete \(\mathscr F_n\)-measurable event \(\mathcal G\) such that, writing \(X=\mu _nZ_{n,N}1\) and \(Y=\widehat \mu _nZ_{n,N}1\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb{P} (\mathcal G^c)\le C/L,\quad \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}H(\eta )]\le C/L,\quad \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}(X^2+Y^2)]\le CL\,.\tag{2.9}\end{equation}

The proof in fact gives \(\mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}\left\|\eta \right\|_{n,-\alpha }]\le CL^{-3/2}\). We need only the weaker response bound in (2.9).

For completeness, the exact grid used in the appendix is chosen by

\begin{equation} \begin {aligned} J&=\left \lfloor \frac L{2\log \rho _0}\right \rfloor ,\qquad \varrho =e^{L/(2J)}\in [\rho _0,\rho _0^2],\\ K&=\left \lfloor \frac {\log N}{\log \varrho }\right \rfloor +1,\qquad N_i=[N\varrho ^{i-K}]\quad (0<i<K)\,. \end {aligned}\tag{2.10}\end{equation}

Set \(N_0=0,N_K=N\). Then \(2J\le K-1\), \(N_{K-2J}=m\) and \(N_{K-J}=n\). The constants in the appendix are common over \(\varrho \in [\rho _0,\rho _0^2]\). The condition \(L\le \log N\) is part of the estimate, and must not be dropped.

First collision, (2.2), and (2.1) also give

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} Z_{0,N}(x_N,1)^{-1}\le C\log N,\qquad \mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{M}} _{N,\delta }^x(1)^{-1}\le C(1+L)\,.\tag{2.11}\end{equation}

Indeed a heat-started second moment is \(1+\sum _{u=m+1}^Nq_{2u}(0)\kappa _N(N-u)\le C(1+\log (N/m))\). For \(B=q_m^{(x_N)}Z_{m,n}1\), the sharper small-horizon bound gives

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} B^{-1}\le \mathbb{E} B^2\le 1+C\frac {\log (n/m)}{\log (1+N/n)}\le C\,.\tag{2.12}\end{equation}

All these bounds hold throughout (2.6) after increasing the common threshold.

3 The fixed-cutoff path limit

This section proves the path-space assertion needed at a fixed positive cutoff. Endpoint-flow convergence alone does not imply this assertion: the diagonal composition at an intermediate time is not continuous for vague convergence. We control that composition in the second moment.

We retain the joint endpoint-flow convergence and mixed second-moment limits of [2, Theorems 1.1 and 9.1, equations (3.51)–(3.56)], in the coordinates and critical window of [1, Theorem A]. The mixed limit is the full two-particle delta-Bose semigroup \(\mathcal Q_2^\theta (t)\), with \(\theta =\vartheta -\gamma _{\mathrm E}\). Its full, rather than centered, moment formula, boundedness, self-adjointness, and semigroup property are the inputs in [7, equations (1.2), (2.13), (2.16) and the text following (2.16)]. In particular, its free two-particle heat term is included. All times in the discrete arguments below are even integers.

3.1 The pair transfer and its uniform bound

For this subsection work first on \(\ell ^2(\mathbb Z^2\times \mathbb Z^2)\) with counting measure. Let \(P_1\) be the one-step walk operator, put \(P=P_1\otimes P_1\), and let \(D\) be multiplication by \(\mathbf 1_{\{z_1=z_2\}}\). Write

\begin{equation} V_N=I+\nu _ND,\qquad A_N=PV_N,\qquad S_N=V_N^{1/2}PV_N^{1/2}.\tag{3.1}\end{equation}

The matrix of \(A_N^m\) is the expectation of the product of two polymer kernels of duration \(m\). Indeed, at each step the two walks first move; the expectation of their two new weights is \(1+\nu _N\) if they meet and is \(1\) otherwise. This proves the assertion by conditioning successively on the steps. The convention excludes the initial noise and includes the terminal noise. Moreover,

\begin{equation} A_N^m=V_N^{-1/2}S_N^mV_N^{1/2}.\tag{3.2}\end{equation}

Lemma 3.1. There is \(c<\infty \) such that, for all sufficiently large \(N\) and every \(m\geq 0\),

\begin{equation} \|S_N^m\|_{2\to 2}\leq e^{cm/N},\qquad \|A_N^m-S_N^m\|_{2\to 2}\leq C\nu _N e^{cm/N}.\tag{3.3}\end{equation}
The constants can be chosen uniformly when the critical parameters belong to a fixed compact interval.

Proof. The planar return estimates give \(R_N=\pi ^{-1}\log N+O(1)\) and \(q_{2j}(0)\leq (\pi j)^{-1}\). Hence, for a sufficiently large fixed \(c\), with \(a=e^{-c/N}\),

\begin{equation} \nu _N^{-1}-\sum _{j\geq 1}a^jq_{2j}(0)\geq 2.\tag{3.4}\end{equation}
To see the uniformity, critical tuning gives \(\nu _N^{-1}=\pi ^{-1}\log N+O(1)\) uniformly on that compact interval, whereas the sum is at most \(-\pi ^{-1}\log (1-e^{-c/N}) =\pi ^{-1}(\log N-\log c)+o(1)\).

Put \(\lambda =a^{-1}>1\). On the collision subspace the kernel of \(DP^jD\) is \(q_j(y-x)^2\). Its row sum is \(q_{2j}(0)\), so the resolvent series and the Schur bound imply

\begin{equation} \|D(\lambda -P)^{-1}D\| \leq \lambda ^{-1}\sum _{j\geq 0}a^jq_{2j}(0).\tag{3.5}\end{equation}
Since \(\lambda -P\) is strictly positive, the elementary quadratic-form bound for a positive perturbation now gives
\begin{equation} (\lambda -P)-\frac {\lambda \nu _N}{1+\nu _N}D\geq 0.\tag{3.6}\end{equation}
For completeness, this bound follows by writing \(D=(\lambda -P)^{1/2}B^*B(\lambda -P)^{1/2}\) with \(B=D(\lambda -P)^{-1/2}\); the required inequality is \(\lambda \nu _N\|BB^*\|/(1+\nu _N)\leq 1\). It holds by (3.5) and (3.4). Conjugating (3.6) by \(V_N^{1/2}\) shows \(S_N\leq \lambda I\).

This also controls the negative spectral edge. The matrix entries of \(S_N\) are nonnegative, whence, for every complex \(u\), \(|\langle u,S_Nu\rangle | \leq \langle |u|,S_N|u|\rangle \leq \lambda \|u\|_2^2\). Self-adjointness therefore gives \(\|S_N\|\leq \lambda \). The first bound follows by taking powers. The second follows from (3.2) and \(\|V_N^{1/2}-I\|+\|V_N^{-1/2}-I\|\leq C\nu _N\). □

Let \(E=\{z\in \mathbb Z^2:z_1+z_2\text { is even}\}\) and \(v_N=4/N\). Use the parity cells in the definition of the rescaled endpoint flow, and let \(J_N:\ell ^2(E,v_N)\longrightarrow L^2(\mathbb R^2)\) extend a function as a constant on each cell. Then \(J_N\) is an isometry, \(J_N^*\) takes cell averages, \(J_N^*J_N=I\), and \(J_NJ_N^*\longrightarrow I\) strongly. The last assertion follows first for continuous compact functions, whose cell averages converge in \(L^2\), and then by density. Use \(\mathcal J_N=J_N\otimes J_N\) on the pair space; its cell volume is \(v_N^2\). Even powers in (3.1) preserve \(E\times E\), and the bounds above hold for their restrictions to this space. Intermediate odd times remain in the operator powers.

3.2 From weak mixed moments to strong pair convergence

Lemma 3.2. For every even sequence \(m_N\) with \(m_N/N\longrightarrow t>0\),

\begin{equation} \mathcal J_N A_N^{m_N}\mathcal J_N^* \longrightarrow \mathcal Q_2^\theta (t) \quad \hbox {strongly on }L^2(\mathbb R^4).\tag{3.7}\end{equation}

Proof. The cited mixed second-moment formula identifies the weak limit on compact tensor tests. Here is the mixed-test point that is sometimes hidden when the source is stated for a variance. Subtract the free product of means. In the exact first/last-collision representation of the interaction covariance, the replicas share their first contact site and their last contact site. Its form \(\mathcal C_N(a_1,a_2;b_1,b_2)\) is thus symmetric and bilinear separately in the incoming pair and the outgoing pair. Consequently,

\begin{equation} \mathcal C_N(a_1,a_2;b_1,b_2) =\frac 1{16}\sum _{\epsilon ,\eta \in \{-1,1\}}\epsilon \eta \, \operatorname {Var} Z_N(a_1+\epsilon a_2,b_1+\eta b_2).\tag{3.8}\end{equation}
This is polarization of the interaction form, rather than an asserted separate symmetry of the full second moment. The free term is added back. Equivalently, one can insert the four tests directly in the positive renewal formula of [2, equations (3.51)–(3.56)]; Cauchy–Schwarz against that renewal measure controls the mixed errors by the corresponding square-test errors. This establishes weak convergence on a dense set of \(L^2\) tensor tests.

This also covers the moving critical parameter \(\vartheta _N\to \vartheta \). Indeed, for nonnegative compact tensor tests the exact two-walk expectation is increasing in \(\nu _N\), by its nonnegative collision expansion. Eventually its value for \(\vartheta _N\) is between its values for the two fixed parameters \(\vartheta -\epsilon \) and \(\vartheta +\epsilon \). The limiting two-particle form is continuous in the parameter: in its positive renewal formula this follows by dominated convergence on a compact parameter interval; equivalently, use the Gamma integral for the interaction kernel in [7, equation (2.4)]. First take \(N\to \infty \) and then \(\epsilon \downarrow 0\). Decomposition into positive and negative parts gives signed compact tests. This argument applies at both \(t\) and \(2t\) and requires no moving-parameter operator theorem.

There is no difficulty from changing an excluded final layer to an included final layer. Indeed, for cell averages (or source evaluations) \(f_N,g_N\) of fixed compact continuous tests \(f,g\), the squared \(L^2\) error of that change is

\begin{equation} \nu _N\langle f_N^{\otimes 2}, A_N^{m-1}P d_N\rangle _{\ell ^2(E\times E,v_N^2)},\qquad d_N(z_1,z_2)=\mathbf 1_{\{z_1=z_2\}}g_N(z_1)^2.\tag{3.9}\end{equation}
This is the conditional variance of the centered last layer; the preceding noise is independent of that layer. Since \(\|d_N\|_2^2=v_N^2\sum _z|g_N(z)|^4=O(v_N)\), Lemma 3.1 bounds (3.9) by \(C\nu _Nv_N^{1/2}\|f_N\|_2^2\longrightarrow 0\). The same pair bound controls the unmodified tested masses in \(L^2\), so it also transfers the mixed moments. Cell averages can replace source evaluations by \(L^2\) approximation and this uniform bound.

Initially take the source’s fixed rounded times. By (3.3), the operators \(T_N(t)=\mathcal J_NS_N^{m_N}\mathcal J_N^*\) are uniformly bounded. The weak convergence on dense tests extends to all \(L^2\) tests. For a fixed \(u\in L^2(\mathbb R^4)\) the isometry gives

\begin{align} \|T_N(t)u\|_2^2 &=\langle u,\mathcal J_NS_N^{2m_N}\mathcal J_N^*u\rangle \longrightarrow \langle u,\mathcal Q_2^\theta (2t)u\rangle =\|\mathcal Q_2^\theta (t)u\|_2^2.\tag{3.10}\end{align}

The weak moment limit at \(2t\) uses the same \(N\)-indexed disorder and the same critical parameter; it does not retune the variance at horizon \(2N\). Weak convergence and (3.10) give strong convergence. Equation (3.3) transfers it to \(A_N\).

Finally the even rounding can vary with \(N\). For even positive \(m,n\) in \([t_0N,TN]\), spectral calculus gives

\begin{equation} \|S_N^m-S_N^n\|\leq C_{c,t_0,T}\frac {|m-n|}{N}.\tag{3.11}\end{equation}
In fact, even powers remove the sign of a spectral value. On \(0<r\leq 1\), \(r^{t_0N}|\log r|\leq (e t_0N)^{-1}\); on \(1\leq r\leq e^{c/N}\) use \(r^{TN}\log r\leq e^{cT}c/N\). The mean-value bound in the exponent proves (3.11). An \(o(N)\) change in the rounded times therefore costs \(o(1)\) in operator norm. This proves the assertion for every stated sequence. □
3.3 Marked cylinders and the interface approximation

Fix a finite grid \(t_0<\cdots <t_d\) with positive spacings. Let \(K_{i,N}\) be the actual random polymer transfer on its \(i\)th slab. Choose \(f_0,\ldots ,f_d\in C_c(\mathbb R^2)\) and put \(f_{i,N}=J_N^*f_i\). Start with nonnegative functions. The raw marked scalar is

\begin{equation} C_N=\langle f_{0,N},K_{1,N}M_{f_{1,N}}K_{2,N}\cdots M_{f_{d-1,N}}K_{d,N}f_{d,N}\rangle _{\ell ^2(E,v_N)}.\tag{3.12}\end{equation}

Choose an even, nonnegative, smooth compactly supported probability mollifier \(u_\ell \), with its scale tending to zero. Let \(U_\ell \) be convolution by \(u_\ell \) and let \(U_{\ell ,N}=J_N^*U_\ell J_N\). The continuum operators are contractions and converge strongly to the identity as \(\ell \to \infty \). For fixed \(\ell \), their discretizations converge strongly to \(U_\ell \) after embedding as \(N\to \infty \). Define \(C_{N,\ell }\) by inserting \(U_{\ell ,N}\) after every internal multiplication in (3.12).

Lemma 3.3. For the preceding compact marked cylinders,

\begin{equation} \lim _{\ell \to \infty }\limsup _{N\to \infty } \mathbb E|C_N-C_{N,\ell }|^2=0.\tag{3.13}\end{equation}
For each fixed \(\ell \), the joint endpoint-flow limit identifies \(C_{N,\ell }\) with the corresponding smoothed marked SHF product. As \(\ell \to \infty \), that product converges in \(L^2\) to the cylinder integral of the associated continuum polymer measure.

Proof. Independence of the slabs within this grid gives exact second-moment identities. The three terms \(\mathbb E C_N^2\), \(\mathbb E(C_NC_{N,\ell })\), and \(\mathbb E C_{N,\ell }^2\) are complete products of the pair transfers. At interface \(i\) their respective insertions, in order, are

\begin{equation} (M_{f_{i,N}}\otimes M_{f_{i,N}}),\qquad (M_{f_{i,N}}\otimes M_{f_{i,N}})(I\otimes U_{\ell ,N}),\qquad (M_{f_{i,N}}\otimes M_{f_{i,N}}) (U_{\ell ,N}\otimes U_{\ell ,N}).\tag{3.14}\end{equation}
They have roots \(f_{0,N}^{\otimes 2},f_{d,N}^{\otimes 2}\) and between insertions the pair transfer \(A_N^{m_{i,N}}\). These formulas follow by expanding the two finite walk sums and averaging each independent slab. Swapping both replicas shows \(\mathbb E(C_NC_{N,\ell })=\mathbb E(C_{N,\ell }C_N)\). For one interface the square-error formula is, for example,
\begin{equation} \mathbb E|C_N-C_{N,\ell }|^2 =\langle f_{0,N}^{\otimes 2},A_N^{m_{1,N}} (M_{f_{1,N}}\otimes M_{f_{1,N}}) ((I-U_{\ell ,N})\otimes (I-U_{\ell ,N})) A_N^{m_{2,N}}f_{2,N}^{\otimes 2}\rangle .\tag{3.15}\end{equation}
For several interfaces use the three complete products (3.14); a product of separate one-interface errors is not the square-error identity.

For fixed \(\ell \), Lemma 3.2, the strong convergence of the cell-average multiplication and convolution operators, and their uniform bounds pass each complete product to its continuum counterpart. Products preserve strong convergence under uniform bounds, as follows by telescoping a finite product on a fixed vector. Sending \(\ell \to \infty \) then makes the three limiting products identical. Their combination with coefficients \(1,-2,1\) proves (3.13).

At fixed \(\ell \) the endpoint-measure functional is continuous for vague convergence after the stated compact restrictions. More explicitly, the connector density between cells is

\begin{equation} \frac 1{v_N^2}\int _{C_N(z)}\int _{C_N(z')} u_\ell (y-y')\,dy\,dy',\tag{3.16}\end{equation}
which converges locally uniformly to \(u_\ell \). Every hidden coordinate is in a fixed compact set: each left interface coordinate is in the support of its compact mark, its right partner is within the fixed support radius of the connector, and the two outer coordinates are in the supports of \(f_0,f_d\). Thus the tested product has compact support on the full product space. Tensor products of finitely many locally finite positive measures converge against continuous compact tests under vague convergence. This proves the asserted endpoint-flow identification; no unproved global continuity of an untruncated composition is used.

The last assertion is precisely the marked-product limit of [7, Definition 1.1, Proposition 2.4, equations (2.30)–(2.33)]. Our compact probability mollifiers are \(L^2\)-applicable: Young’s inequality makes their convolution operators contractions and approximation of the identity gives strong convergence. Heat-kernel mollifiers are also \(L^2\)-applicable. The proposition makes the marked limit independent of that choice. It therefore identifies the compact mollifier construction with the heat-regularized marked construction of [4, Theorem 2.13], on the same endpoint-flow realization. This is the required compact/heat interface identification. In particular, no assertion that every \(L^2\) convolution kernel acts boundedly on \(L^2\) is needed. Signed marks follow by finite linear combinations of nonnegative ones. □

The argument also proves joint convergence of finitely many marked integrals together with any prescribed finite family of endpoint kernels. For fixed smoothing all these integrals are functions of the finite union of their endpoint arrays. Their individual \(L^2\) approximation errors control the error of the vector by a finite sum. Intervals belonging to different grids or cutoffs may overlap; they are never declared independent. Independence was used only within each disjoint grid when proving its individual moment identity.

3.4 The heat root, the Brownian prefix, and normalization

Fix \(\delta \in (0,1)\) and a deterministic \(x\in \mathbb R^2\). Let \(m=[\delta N]\) be the even ceiling and let \(x_N\in E\) be the chosen approximation to \(\sqrt {N/2}\,x\). Erasing the weights through time \(m\) gives the exact incoming probability row \(q_m(\cdot -x_N)\). Its density in the preceding Hilbert-space convention is

\begin{equation} r_{N,\delta }(z)=q_m(z-x_N)/v_N,\qquad J_Nr_{N,\delta }\longrightarrow g_\delta (\cdot -x) \quad \hbox {in }L^1\cap L^2.\tag{3.17}\end{equation}

This is the parity local central limit theorem. In particular, the root has bounded \(L^2\) norm for each fixed \(\delta \).

Denote the erased random finite path measure by

\begin{equation} \widehat{\mathsf{M}} _{N,\delta }^x(F) =E_{x_N}\left [F(X_N)\prod _{j=m+1}^NW_{j,S_j}\right ].\tag{3.18}\end{equation}

Let \(\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x\) be the continuum polymer measure on \([\delta ,1]\), weighted at its starting position by \(g_\delta (\cdot -x)\), with the conditional Brownian bridge from \(x\) to that position attached on \([0,\delta ]\). This definition uses the measurable continuous-path construction of [4, Theorems 2.10, 2.13 and Proposition 2.14], with the normalization and parameter dictionary stated above. Its first moment is the Wiener law \(P_x^{\rm BM}\), because the unweighted continuum measure has Lebesgue-start Wiener first moment and Brownian disintegration at time \(\delta \) has density \(g_\delta \).

Proposition 3.4. Jointly for any finite prescribed family of cutoffs and deterministic starting points,

\begin{equation} \widehat{\mathsf{M}} _{N,\delta }^x\ \Longrightarrow \ \widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x \quad \hbox {in the weak topology of finite path measures},\qquad \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _{N,\delta }^x\ \Longrightarrow \ \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x,\tag{3.19}\end{equation}
where each \(\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} \) is the corresponding measure divided by its total mass. The convergence holds jointly with every prescribed finite family of endpoint kernels, using a single limiting SHF for all the cutoffs and starting points.

Proof. First truncate the heat root and the terminal weight \(1\) continuously on compact sets. For these truncations, (3.17) and the uniform pair bound allow \(L^2\) approximation by compact continuous roots in the marked-cylinder lemma. Removing the root truncation and terminal truncation costs an \(L^1\) error bounded by the corresponding ordinary walk, or Brownian, tail: the measures are positive and their annealed first moments are the ordinary walk and Wiener laws. Thus all bounded compact marked cylinders after \(\delta \) converge jointly as asserted.

Marks at or before \(\delta \) are absorbed into the deterministic incoming row. For a finite product of bounded continuous such marks this row is bounded in absolute value by a constant times \(q_m(\cdot -x_N)\). The deterministic local central limit theorem and the Markov property give its limiting heat row with the appropriate Brownian-bridge conditional expectation. The domination by the heat row, with standard Gaussian walk bounds at fixed positive duration, gives the needed \(L^1\) and \(L^2\) convergence. This proves cylinder convergence on the whole interval \([0,1]\).

Actual interpolation times may replace their even ceilings. They differ by at most two walk steps, so the spatial discrepancy at each of a fixed finite list of times is deterministically \(O(N^{-1/2})\). Uniform continuity on compact sets, followed by the annealed tail bound, removes this discrepancy and the replacement of cell averages by point evaluations. No prior polymer time modulus is used here.

For tightness, the annealed measure in (3.18) is exactly the scaled walk law. Therefore, for every compact path set \(K\) and every \(\epsilon >0\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb P\{\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _{N,\delta }^x(K^c)>\epsilon \} \leq \epsilon ^{-1}P_{x_N}\{X_N\notin K\},\qquad \mathbb E\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _{N,\delta }^x(1)=1.\tag{3.20}\end{equation}
Donsker tightness, a sequence of compact sets with summably small right-hand sides, and Markov’s inequality prove tightness of the laws of these random finite measures. Finite products of these arguments give joint tightness. Joint cylinder integrals at rational times, including the total mass, identify every subsequential limit; continuous paths and a countable determining class make this identification an identification of random-measure laws. The preceding joint endpoint-array argument proves the final joint assertion too.

Finally

\begin{equation} W_\delta ^x=\widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x(1) =\mathscr Z_{\delta ,1}^\vartheta (g_\delta (\cdot -x),1) \quad \hbox {satisfies}\quad 0<W_\delta ^x<\infty \quad \hbox {a.s.}\tag{3.21}\end{equation}
Finiteness follows from its mean \(1\). For positivity choose a nonzero nonnegative compact product test below \(g_\delta (\cdot -x)\otimes 1\) and apply [5, Corollary 1.3]; that test has positive Wiener integral. The normalization map \(\mu \mapsto \mu /\mu (1)\) is continuous in the weak finite-measure topology at positive total mass. The continuous mapping theorem proves the probability-measure assertion. □

The preceding result retains the existing SHF convergence theorem and the existing unpinned continuum path measure. Its additional content is the discrete marked-interface argument. It does not remove the cutoff or supply an initial-time modulus for the original point-start polymer; those are separate steps.

4 Cancellation for tail paths

A deterministic observable of \((S_k,\ldots ,S_N)\) permits a first-noise expansion through time \(k\). Expanding beyond \(k\) would retain an unrecorded path prefix in the observable. Keeping the original response horizon \(N\) also makes the smoothing constant uniform in \(k\).

Proposition 4.1. Under (2.6), for every integer \(\max (16,4n)\le k\le N\) and every deterministic \(F=F(S_k,\ldots ,S_N)\) with \(|F|\le 1\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} |\mathsf{Q} _N^x(F)-\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _{N,\delta }^x(F)|\le C\rho (L),\qquad \rho (L)=L^{-1/12}(\log L)^{1/6}+L^{-1}\,.\tag{4.1}\end{equation}
The constant is independent of \(k,x,N,\delta \) in this range. The supremum over deterministic tests can be taken outside the expectation.

Proof. For the past-measurable row \(\eta \) in (2.7), expand \(W=1+\xi \) only through \(k\) and group by the first selected noise:

\begin{align} Z_{n,N}(\eta ;F)&=A_F+B_F,\nonumber \\ A_F&=\sum _{r=1}^{k-n}\sum _z(\eta q_r)(z)\xi _{n+r,z}Z_{n+r,N}(z;F),\qquad B_F=Z_{k,N}(\eta q_{k-n};F)\,.\tag{4.2}\end{align}

The suffix at \(n+r=k\) excludes noise at \(k\), so no layer is counted twice. The observable has no dependence on the path before \(k\), which justifies the heat prefix in each term.

Conditionally on \(\mathscr F_n\), summands with different first-noise times are orthogonal: integrate the centered noise at the earlier time. Different sites at the same time are also orthogonal, and all suffixes use strictly later noise. Since \(|Z_{u,N}(z;F)|\le Z_{u,N}(z,1)\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} [|A_F|^2\mid \mathscr F_n]\le H(\eta )^2,\qquad \mathbb{E} [|B_F|\mid \mathscr F_n]\le \left\|\eta q_{k-n}\right\|_1\,.\tag{4.3}\end{equation}
For integers \(k/4\le r\le k/2\), the hypotheses imply \(r\le k-n\) and \(N-n-r\ge N/4\). The \(\ell ^2\) contraction of convolution and (2.4) give
\begin{equation} H(\eta )^2\ge ck\left\|\eta q_{k-n}\right\|_2^2\,.\tag{4.4}\end{equation}
There are at least \(k/8\) such integers. In a ball \(D_R=\{|z-x_N|\le R\sqrt k\}\), Cauchy–Schwarz therefore bounds the \(\ell ^1\) difference by \(CRH(\eta )\).

Put \(p=\mu _n q_{k-n}\) and \(\widehat p=\widehat \mu _nq_{k-n}\). Equal total mass yields

\begin{equation*} \left\|p-\widehat p\right\|_1\le 2\sum _{D_R}|p-\widehat p|+2\widehat p(D_R^c)\,. \end{equation*}
If \(C_R=q_m^{(x_N)}Z_{m,n}(q_{k-n}\mathbf{1}_{D_R^c})\), then \(0\le C_R\le B\), \(\widehat p(D_R^c)=C_R/B\) and \(\mathbb{E} C_R=q_k^{(x_N)}(D_R^c)\le Ce^{-cR^2}\). Using (2.12),
\begin{equation*} \mathbb{E} \widehat p(D_R^c)\le (\mathbb{E} C_R\,\mathbb{E} B^{-1})^{1/2}\le Ce^{-cR^2}\,. \end{equation*}
This follows from \(C_R/B^2\le B^{-1}\) and Cauchy–Schwarz; independence is not needed. Combining with (2.9) and (4.3), and choosing \(R=A\sqrt {\log L}\), gives
\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}|Z_{n,N}(\eta ;F)|]\le CL^{-1}\sqrt {\log L}\,.\tag{4.5}\end{equation}

Let \(X_F=Z_{n,N}(\mu _n;F)\) and \(Y_F=Z_{n,N}(\widehat \mu _n;F)\). The Markov property gives \(\mathsf{Q} _N^x(F)=X_F/X\) and \(\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _{N,\delta }^x(F)=Y_F/Y\). Positivity gives \(|X_F|\le X\), \(|Y_F|\le Y\). For \(D_F=|X_F-Y_F|+|X-Y|\),

\begin{equation} |X_F/X-Y_F/Y|\le \min (2,D_F/X)\le 2^{2/3}D_F^{1/3}X^{-1/3}\,.\tag{4.6}\end{equation}
Condition on \(\mathscr F_n\) in (2.1) with \(p=1/2\). Since \(\mathcal G\) is measurable there, concavity and (2.9) give
\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}X^{-1/2}]\le \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}(\mathbb{E} [X^2\mid \mathscr F_n])^{3/8}] \le (\mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}X^2])^{3/8}\le (CL)^{3/8}\,.\tag{4.7}\end{equation}
Apply (4.5) to \(F\) and to \(1\), then use Hölder in (4.6). The good-event contribution is at most
\begin{equation*} C(L^{-1}\sqrt {\log L})^{1/3}(L^{3/8})^{2/3} =CL^{-1/12}(\log L)^{1/6}\,. \end{equation*}
The complement contributes at most \(2\mathbb{P} (\mathcal G^c)\le C/L\). This proves the proposition. □

This is not an expected total-variation bound: the supremum has not been moved inside \(\mathbb{E} \). For a functional of the interpolated path on \([a,1]\), take \(k=\lfloor aN\rfloor \), which retains the segment crossing \(a\). If \(\sqrt \delta \le a/8\) and \(L\ge L_0\), all hypotheses hold for \(N\ge \max (N_*,16,e^L,40/a)\).

5 Removing the continuum cutoff

We first compare cutoffs on each fixed positive-time interval. Their joint identification from Section 3 is essential: a comparison of unrelated marginal laws would not construct a random measure on the given flow space.

Fix \(a>0\). Two applications of Proposition 4.1 through the same \(\mathsf{Q} _N^x\), followed by the joint fixed-cutoff limit, give

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} |\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^xF-\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^xF| \le C\{\rho (|\log \delta |)+\rho (|\log \epsilon |)\}\tag{5.1}\end{equation}

for every deterministic \(F\in C_b(C([a,1],\mathbb{R} ^2))\), \(|F|\le 1\), when both logarithms are at least \(L_0\) and \(\sqrt \delta ,\sqrt \epsilon \le a/8\). For this fixed pair, first take \(N\ge \max (N_*,16,\delta ^{-1},\epsilon ^{-1},40/a)\) and then \(N\to \infty \). The difference of evaluations is a bounded continuous function on the pair of probability spaces, so expectation passes to the limit. Approximation in \(L^1(\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x+\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^x)\) extends the inequality to each fixed bounded Borel test.

Lemma 5.1. On \(C([a,1],\mathbb{R} ^2)\), the restricted \(\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x\) are Cauchy in expected Wasserstein distance for cost \(d_a(\gamma ,\gamma ')=1\wedge \left\|\gamma -\gamma '\right\|_\infty \).

Proof. Choose one sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon \) so the right side of (5.1) is small for all smaller cutoffs. The annealed probability \(\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^x\) is tight. Applying the Borel version of the inequality to the complement of a compact set proves annealed tightness uniformly for sufficiently small cutoffs.

On a deterministic compact set \(K\), the bounded one-Lipschitz functions have a finite uniform \(\eta \)-net, by Arzelà–Ascoli. Use (5.1) on its finitely many representatives \(F_1,\ldots ,F_M\). The expected Wasserstein distance is bounded by

\begin{equation*} \sum _{i=1}^M\mathbb{E} |\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^xF_i-\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^xF_i| +2\eta +2\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x(K^c)+2\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^x(K^c)\,. \end{equation*}
First send the cutoffs to zero with the compact set and net fixed; then send \(\eta \) to zero. This proves the claim. The Wasserstein metric for the complete bounded cost \(d_a\) is complete and metrizes weak convergence. A subsequence with summable consecutive expected distances is almost surely Cauchy; the full family then converges in expected distance. Its limit is measurable on the same flow space. □

We now control the complete continuum path, rather than merely its restrictions. The Wiener first moment of the unnormalized measure gives \(\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x=P_x^{\rm BM}\). Passing (2.11) at fixed \(\delta \) by the lower semicontinuity of \(w\mapsto w^{-1}\) gives \(\mathbb{E} (W_\delta ^x)^{-1}\le C(1+|\log \delta |)\). For any Borel event \(A\), since \(0\le \widehat{\mathsf{M}} _\delta ^x(A)\le W_\delta ^x\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x(A)\le \{C(1+|\log \delta |)P_x^{\rm BM}(A)\}^{1/2}\,.\tag{5.2}\end{equation}

Indeed \(V/W\le \sqrt {V/W}\) when \(0\le V\le W\), and Cauchy–Schwarz applies to \(\sqrt V\sqrt {W^{-1}}\).

Lemma 5.2. For each \(r>0\),

\begin{equation} \lim _{a\downarrow 0}\limsup _{\delta \downarrow 0} \mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x\{\sup _{t\le a}|X_t-x|>r\}=0\,.\tag{5.3}\end{equation}

Proof. Put \(L=|\log \delta |\), \(a_j=2^{-j}\), and choose \(b=a_{\lfloor \log _2L\rfloor }\in [1/L,2/L]\). Brownian maximal bounds and (5.2) bound the initial interval \([0,b]\) by \(C\sqrt L e^{-cr^2L}\). Cover the rest of \([0,2a_J]\) by \([a_j,2a_j]\), \(J\le j\le \lfloor \log _2L\rfloor \). On the \(j\)th annulus compare with the cutoff \(\epsilon _j=e^{-1/a_j}\) using (5.1). For sufficiently small fixed outer scale \(a_J\), \(\sqrt {\epsilon _j}\le a_j/8\) for every \(j\ge J\). For large \(L\), also \(\sqrt \delta \le 1/(8L)\le a_j/8\) for every annulus used. Thus the entire excursion probability is at most

\begin{equation} C\sqrt L e^{-cr^2L}+C(1+\log L)\rho (L) +C\sum _{j\ge J}\left \{\rho (1/a_j)+a_j^{-1/2}e^{-cr^2/a_j}\right \}\,.\tag{5.4}\end{equation}
The second term tends to zero. Both series converge and their tails tend to zero, since \(\rho (1/a_j)=a_j^{1/12}(\log (1/a_j))^{1/6}+a_j\). This proves the lemma, taking \(L\to \infty \) before \(J\to \infty \). □

Let \(T_a\) extend a tail path by linear interpolation from \(x\) at time \(0\) to its value at \(a\). It is one-Lipschitz for the bounded uniform metrics. A path starting at \(x\) differs from this extension by at most \(2\sup _{t\le a}|X_t-x|\). Consequently the full Wasserstein distance \(\mathsf W_0\) satisfies

\begin{align*} \mathbb{E} \mathsf W_0(\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x,\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^x) &\le 4r+\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x\{\sup _{t\le a}|X_t-x|>r\} +\mathbb{E} \widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^x\{\sup _{t\le a}|X_t-x|>r\}\\ &\quad +\mathbb{E} \mathsf W_a(\operatorname{res} _a\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x,\operatorname{res} _a\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\epsilon ^x)\,. \end{align*}

First send \(\delta ,\epsilon \downarrow 0\), then \(a\downarrow 0\), and then \(r\downarrow 0\). Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2 show that the full family is Cauchy in expected \(\mathsf W_0\). Its limit \(\mathsf{Q} ^x\) is a measurable random probability on \(\Gamma \). The set \(\{X(0)=x\}\) is closed and has full mass for every cutoff, so it also has full mass for the limit. This proves the first assertion of Theorem 1.1. This direct full-path Cauchy argument avoids a separate projective-extension construction.

6 The discrete prefix and the lattice limit

The discrete initial segment still needs its own estimate. A sequence can converge on every fixed positive-time tail while making an excursion at times tending to zero. Here the common finite-lattice range (2.6) permits the required growing family of cutoffs.

Proposition 6.1. For each \(r>0\),

\begin{equation} \lim _{a\downarrow 0}\limsup _{N\to \infty } \mathbb{E} \mathsf{Q} _N^x\{\sup _{0\le t\le a}|X_N(t)-x|>r\}=0\,.\tag{6.1}\end{equation}

Proof. For any walk event \(A\), the numerator mass \(V=\mathsf{M} _N^x(A)\) has \(\mathbb{E} V=P_{x_N}(A)\) and \(0\le V\le Z_{0,N}(x_N,1)\). The argument for (5.2) and (2.11) give

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} \mathsf{Q} _N^x(A)\le \{C\log N\,P_{x_N}(A)\}^{1/2}\,.\tag{6.2}\end{equation}
Put \(b_N=(\log \log N)^{-2}\). Walk maximal bounds, with \(|\sqrt {2/N}\,x_N-x|=O(N^{-1/2})\) and interpolation errors absorbed, bound an excursion on \([0,b_N]\) by
\begin{equation*} C\sqrt {\log N}\exp (-cr^2/b_N)\longrightarrow 0\,. \end{equation*}

Fix a small outer dyadic scale \(a_J=2^{-J}\) and cover \([b_N,2a_J]\) by the annuli \([a_j,2a_j]\), \(J\le j\le j_N\), with \(a_{j_N}\in (b_N/2,b_N]\). For each annulus choose \(\delta _j=e^{-1/a_j}\), so \(L_j=1/a_j\). Eventually,

\begin{equation} L_0\le L_j\le 2(\log \log N)^2\le \log N\,.\tag{6.3}\end{equation}
Take the outer scale small enough that \(e^{-1/(2a_j)}\le a_j/8\) for every \(j\ge J\). As \(Nb_N\to \infty \), the integers \(k_j=\lfloor a_jN\rfloor \) simultaneously satisfy \(k_j\ge 16\) and \(k_j\ge 4[\sqrt {\delta _j}N]\). Indeed \(4[\sqrt {\delta _j}N]\le a_jN/2+8\le \lfloor a_jN\rfloor \) once \(a_jN\ge 18\); the smallest \(a_jN\) tends to infinity. Thus Proposition 4.1 applies simultaneously to every annular excursion event, including its interpolation segment.

For the delayed law, the numerator has ordinary walk mean and its inverse mass is at most \(C(1+L_j)\). Therefore its annular excursion contribution is at most \(Ca_j^{-1/2}e^{-cr^2/a_j}\), uniformly in the displayed range. Summing the comparison and delayed contributions gives

\begin{equation*} \limsup _{N\to \infty }\mathbb{E} \mathsf{Q} _N^x\{\sup _{t\le 2a_J}|X_N(t)-x|>r\} \le C\sum _{j\ge J}\{\rho (1/a_j)+a_j^{-1/2}e^{-cr^2/a_j}\}\,. \end{equation*}
The series tail tends to zero as \(J\to \infty \). Together with the initial \(b_N\) estimate, this proves (6.1). □

Proof of Theorem 1.1, lattice convergence. For each fixed \(a>0\), Proposition 4.1 compares the annealed tail law with one fixed-cutoff law with arbitrarily small error, uniformly over deterministic bounded tail tests. Section 3 gives tightness of that fixed-cutoff sequence. Approximation of compact-complement indicators by continuous tests gives annealed tightness on \(C([a,1],\mathbb{R} ^2)\).

Here is the full modulus argument. For \(\epsilon ,\eta >0\), Proposition 6.1 permits a fixed \(a>0\) such that the limiting upper probability of \(\sup _{t\le 2a}|X_N(t)-x|>\epsilon /4\) is below \(\eta \). Tail tightness on \([a,1]\) gives an \(h<a\) for which the limiting upper probability of a tail oscillation exceeding \(\epsilon \) over an interval of length \(h\) is below \(\eta \). A pair of times at distance at most \(h\) whose earlier time is below \(a\) lies in \([0,2a]\); every other pair is controlled by the tail modulus. Thus

\begin{equation*} \lim _{h\downarrow 0}\limsup _{N\to \infty }\mathbb{E} \mathsf{Q} _N^x\{\sup _{|s-t|\le h}|X_N(s)-X_N(t)|>\epsilon \}=0\,. \end{equation*}
The initial values are deterministic and converge to \(x\). Finitely many exceptional \(N\) are handled by decreasing \(h\), since each fixed- \(N\) law is supported on continuous paths. The uniform modulus criterion and Arzelà–Ascoli give tightness of the full annealed path probabilities.

Tightness of annealed probabilities implies tightness of the laws of the random probabilities. To see this, for each \(\epsilon >0\) choose deterministic compact sets \(K_j\) with \(\sup _N\mathbb{E} \mathsf{Q} _N^x(K_j^c)\le \epsilon 2^{-2j}\). Markov’s inequality shows that with probability at least \(1-\epsilon \), \(\mathsf{Q} _N^x(K_j^c)\le 2^{-j}\) for every \(j\). These constraints define a uniformly tight family of probabilities and thus a relatively compact set in \(\mathcal P(\Gamma )\). Finite exceptional values of \(N\) can be included by enlarging the compact sets.

Every subsequential random-probability limit is supported on \(C_x([0,1],\mathbb{R} ^2)\). Indeed evaluation at zero is continuous, and each prelimit initial marginal is the deterministic point mass at \(\sqrt {2/N}\,x_N\to x\). Testing this marginal against a countable determining class proves the support assertion almost surely.

To identify a subsequential law, fix finitely many deterministic bounded continuous tail tests \(F_1,\ldots ,F_d\), possibly on different positive-time intervals and from a finite list of starts. Proposition 4.1 bounds the expected error of every evaluation by \(C\rho (|\log \delta |)\). For fixed \(\delta \), the joint fixed-cutoff limit identifies their evaluations as those of \(\widehat{\mathsf{Q}} _\delta ^x\). Sending \(\delta \downarrow 0\) uses the same-space convergence already proved. The converging-together argument therefore identifies the joint laws of all such evaluations as those of \(\mathsf{Q} ^x\).

Take a countable determining collection on each dyadic tail interval. Their evaluations jointly determine a random probability supported on \(C_x([0,1],\mathbb{R} ^2)\): the restrictions determine every path, and their Borel sigma field is the full one. Hence every subsequential random-probability law is \(\operatorname{Law} (\mathsf{Q} ^x)\). This proves the lattice limit. All estimates and finite-dimensional identifications work on finite products, proving the claimed joint assertion. □

Proof status and scope. The proof imports the previously constructed flow and unpinned path measure, its moment semigroup, and the stated Gaussian, renewal and fourth-moment estimates. It proves the additional path and cutoff arguments above and in the appendix. It is a natural-language deduction from these inputs, not a formal verification. The existing Lean work was not run or resumed. No assertion is made here about deterministic point-to-point pinning, an all-start disintegration specified at every point, a path density relative to Wiener measure, or a conditional multiplicative-chaos relation after removing the cutoff.

A Discrete shape estimates and cancellation

This appendix proves the estimates for the early boundary used in the main argument. The scalar renewal estimates and the fourth-moment theorem are retained from [1, 3]; the coefficient and reset arguments below are project deductions. They require neither convergence of the point-start polymer nor any conclusion about its limiting path law.

Write \(R(h)=\sum _{j=1}^h q_{2j}(0)\), with \(R(0)=0\), and let \(\mathcal S_a=\{z:q_a(z)>0\}\). We work at the origin; translation gives the same estimates at any starting site. All sums with denominator \(q_a(z)\) are over \(\mathcal S_a\). In particular, no division by zero occurs. Fix \(0<\alpha <1\) throughout.

A.1 A norm adapted to the heat kernel

The rotated coordinates \(U(z)=z_1+z_2\) and \(V(z)=z_1-z_2\) of the planar walk are independent sums of independent Rademacher increments. On the one-dimensional \(a\)-step law define the normalized Krawtchouk polynomials by

\begin{equation*} K_k^{(a)}(S_a)=\binom ak^{-1/2} \sum _{\substack {A\subset \{1,\ldots ,a\}\\|A|=k}} \prod _{j\in A}\epsilon _j,\qquad 0\le k\le a. \end{equation*}

The symmetric Walsh polynomials on the right depend only on \(S_a\). Walsh orthogonality shows that they are an orthonormal basis for functions of \(S_a\). Thus \(\psi _{kl}^{(a)}(z)=K_k^{(a)}(U(z))K_l^{(a)}(V(z))\) is an orthonormal basis in \(L^2(q_a)\). For an accessible signed row \(\eta \) put

\begin{equation} c_{kl}^{(a)}(\eta )=\sum _z\eta (z)\psi _{kl}^{(a)}(z),\qquad \mathcal N_{a,\alpha }(\eta )^2 =\sum _{k,l=0}^a(1+k+l)^{-\alpha }|c_{kl}^{(a)}(\eta )|^2.\tag{A.1}\end{equation}

The notation \(\|\eta \|_{a,-\alpha }\) denotes the same expression with \((k,l)=(0,0)\) omitted. For a zero-mass row these norms agree.

Conditional expectation of Walsh monomials gives, for \(a\le v\),

\begin{equation} E\big [K_k^{(v)}(S_v)\mid S_a\big ] =\sqrt {\binom ak/\binom vk}\,K_k^{(a)}(S_a).\tag{A.2}\end{equation}

Here and below a coefficient with unavailable degree is zero. Consequently heat transport multiplies coefficient \((k,l)\) by \(\sqrt {(a)_k(a)_l/((v)_k(v)_l)}\), where \((a)_k\) is a falling factorial. It contracts the squared zero-mass norm from age \(a\) to age \(b\) by at most \(a/b\). It also gives, for \(a<v\le b\),

\begin{equation} f_v:=\sum _z\frac {(\eta q_{v-a})(z)^2}{q_v(z)} \le \sum _{k,l}|c_{kl}^{(a)}(\eta )|^2 e^{-(k+l)(v-a)/b}.\tag{A.3}\end{equation}

These statements allow all accessible rows, including rows supported near the edge of \(\mathcal S_a\).

Lemma A.1 (One-block coefficient estimate). Let \(L>1\), \(1\le a<b\), \(b/a\le L\), \(h=b-a\), and \(\Delta =1-\nu R(h)>0\). Consider independent centered noise variables of variance \(\nu \ge 0\) and the transfer \(T=Z_{a,b}\) with weights \(1+\xi \). Set

\begin{equation*} D=T-(T\mathbf{1})\otimes q_b,\qquad \varepsilon =\frac {\nu }{\Delta } \left (1+\frac {\nu }{\Delta }\right ). \end{equation*}
There is \(C_{\alpha ,L}<\infty \) such that
\begin{align} \mathbb{E} \|\eta D\|_{b,-\alpha }^2 &\le \left (\frac ab+C_{\alpha ,L}\varepsilon \right ) \|\eta \|_{a,-\alpha }^2, &&\eta \mathbf{1}=0,\tag{A.4}\\ \mathbb{E} \|q_aD\|_{b,-\alpha }^2&\le C_{\alpha ,L}\varepsilon ,\tag{A.5}\\ \mathbb{E} |\eta T\mathbf{1}-\eta \mathbf{1}|^2 &\le C_{\alpha ,L}\varepsilon \mathcal N_{a,\alpha }(\eta )^2.\tag{A.6}\end{align}

The constants are independent of \(a,b,\nu \) and \(\eta \).

Proof. Let \(Y_{t-}=\eta Z_{a,t-1}q_1\) and \(F_t=\mathbb{E} \sum _zY_{t-}(z)^2/q_t(z)\). The Doob martingale for \(\eta T f\) has time-\(t\) increment

\begin{equation*} \sum _zY_{t-}(z)\xi _{t,z}(q_{b-t}f)(z). \end{equation*}
Its increments are orthogonal in time and site. Applying the isometry before the time-\(t\) noise gives
\begin{equation} F_t=f_t+\nu \sum _{u=a+1}^{t-1}\sum _v\mathbb{E} Y_{u-}(v)^2 \sum _z\frac {q_{t-u}(z-v)^2}{q_t(z)}.\tag{A.7}\end{equation}
The following collision inequality preserves the critical leading constant:
\begin{equation} q_u(v)\sum _z\frac {q_r(z-v)^2}{q_{u+r}(z)} \le \left (\frac {u+r+1}{u+1}\right )^2q_{2r}(0).\tag{A.8}\end{equation}
To prove it, first use the one-dimensional binomial law and write \(v=u-2k\). Its left side is
\begin{equation*} 2^{-r}\binom uk\sum _{l=0}^r \frac {\binom rl^2}{\binom {u+r}{k+l}}. \end{equation*}
Insert \(\binom mj^{-1}=(m+1)\int _0^1x^j(1-x)^{m-j}\,dx\). The polynomial \(\sum _l\binom rl^2x^l(1-x)^{r-l}\) is bounded by \(2^{-r}\binom {2r}{r}\). Indeed it is the circle average of \((1+2\sqrt {x(1-x)}\cos \theta )^r\); its surviving even-power coefficients are nonnegative, so its maximum is at \(x=1/2\). The remaining beta integral gives the one-dimensional bound with factor \((u+r+1)/(u+1)\). Multiplication of the two independent rotated coordinates proves (A.8).

Divide (A.7) by \((t+1)^2\) before iteration. With \(k(r)=q_{2r}(0)\) and

\begin{equation*} U(0)=1,\qquad U(r)=\sum _{m\ge 1}\nu ^m k^{*m}(r)\quad (r\ge 1), \end{equation*}
finite renewal iteration yields
\begin{equation} F_t\le L^2\sum _{v=a+1}^tU(t-v)f_v,\qquad \sum _{r=0}^hU(r)\le \Delta ^{-1},\qquad U(r)\le \frac {C\nu }{r\Delta ^2}\quad (1\le r\le h).\tag{A.9}\end{equation}
For the last bound use \(r k^{*m}(r)\le m(\sup _s s k(s))R(h)^{m-1}\) and sum the geometric series. The factor \(L^2\) is introduced after the renewal iteration; it is not inserted into the renewal kernel.

Set \(w_t=b^{-\alpha }(1+b-t)^{\alpha -1}\). Splitting the convolution at half its duration in (A.9) gives

\begin{equation*} \sum _{r=0}^dU(r)(1+d-r)^{\alpha -1} \le C_\alpha \Delta ^{-1}(1+\nu /\Delta )(1+d)^{\alpha -1}. \end{equation*}
Here the first half uses the total mass of \(U\), and the second half uses its pointwise bound. Also, for each integer degree \(d\ge 0\),
\begin{equation} b^{-\alpha }\sum _{x=1}^h(1+h-x)^{\alpha -1}e^{-dx/b} \le C_\alpha (1+d)^{-\alpha }.\tag{A.10}\end{equation}
For \(d=0\), sum the power. For \(d\ge 1\) and \(h\le b/(1+d)\), drop the exponential. In the remaining case split at \(h/2\). The first part is at most \(C b^{-\alpha }h^{\alpha -1}(b/d)\); the second is at most \(C(h/b)^\alpha e^{-dh/(2b)}\). Both have the required bound. Using (A.3) now gives
\begin{equation} \nu \sum _{t=a+1}^bw_tF_t \le C_{\alpha ,L}\varepsilon \mathcal N_{a,\alpha }(\eta )^2.\tag{A.11}\end{equation}

We record the diagonal mode estimate needed for the shape output:

\begin{equation} \sum _{k+l\ge 1}(1+k+l)^{-\alpha } \frac {(t)_k(t)_l}{(b)_k(b)_l}\psi _{kl}^{(t)}(z)^2 \le C_{\alpha ,L}\frac {w_t}{q_t(z)}.\tag{A.12}\end{equation}
For completeness, the one-dimensional Rademacher noise operator with correlation \(\zeta \in [0,1]\) has diagonal bound
\begin{equation*} \sum _k\zeta ^kK_k^{(t)}(u)^2 \le \frac {C}{p_t(u)\sqrt {1+t(1-\zeta )}}. \end{equation*}
Conditioning on the input increments, the modulus of the output characteristic function is \((1-(1-\zeta ^2)\sin ^2\theta )^{t/2}\). Fourier inversion bounds each output atom by \(C[1+t(1-\zeta ^2)]^{-1/2}\), which proves the displayed diagonal bound. Multiply the two coordinates, use \((t)_k/(b)_k\le (t/b)^k\), and integrate
\begin{equation*} (1+k+l)^{-\alpha } =\frac 1{\Gamma (\alpha )}\int _0^\infty r^{\alpha -1}e^{-(1+k+l)r}\,dr. \end{equation*}
Since \(t/b\ge 1/L\), the resulting integral is bounded by
\begin{equation*} C_{\alpha ,L}\int _0^\infty \frac {r^{\alpha -1}e^{-r}}{1+b-t+b\min (r,1)}\,dr \le C_{\alpha ,L}b^{-\alpha }(1+b-t)^{\alpha -1}. \end{equation*}
The final step uses \(\int _0^\infty v^{\alpha -1}/(1+v)\,dv<\infty \), and hence needs \(\alpha <1\).

Sum the Doob isometry over the nonconstant terminal modes and use (A.11)–(A.12). The deterministic part is bounded by \(a/b\) on zero-mass rows and vanishes for the heat row \(q_a\); this proves the first two conclusions. For the last conclusion the flat-terminal isometry is

\begin{equation*} \mathbb{E} |\eta T\mathbf{1}-\eta \mathbf{1}|^2 =\nu \sum _{t,z}\mathbb{E} Y_{t-}(z)^2. \end{equation*}
Use \(\max q_t\le C/t\), \(t^{-1}\le L/b\), and \(w_t\ge 1/b\), then (A.11). All sums are finite. The proof also gives the conditional inequalities for a past-measurable input row, with that row held fixed while the independent fresh block is averaged. □
A.2 Uniform reset estimates

Fix \(\rho >24\) and let \(\varrho \in [\rho ,\rho ^2]\). For even \(N\) put

\begin{equation} \begin {aligned} K&=\lfloor \log N/\log \varrho \rfloor +1,\qquad N_0=0,\qquad N_K=N,\\ N_i&=[N\varrho ^{i-K}]\quad (1\le i<K),\qquad j_i=K-i\,. \end {aligned}\tag{A.13}\end{equation}

where \([s]=2\lceil s/2\rceil \). Set \(q_i=q_{N_i}\) and \(T_i=Z_{N_{i-1},N_i}\). For a noninitial block with \(a=N_{i-1}\), \(b=N_i\) and \(h=b-a\), rounding gives

\begin{equation*} b\le 3N\varrho ^{-j_i},\qquad b/a\le 3\rho ^2,\qquad a/b\le 3/\rho . \end{equation*}

Uniformly for critical parameters in a fixed compact interval \(I\),

\begin{equation} \nu _N^{-1}-R(h)=\pi ^{-1}\log (N/h)+O_I(1).\tag{A.14}\end{equation}

Thus, for all \(j_i\ge J_{\rm raw}\) and \(N\ge N_{\rm raw}\), the hypotheses of Lemma A.1 hold and its \(\varepsilon \) is at most \(C/j_i\). These thresholds are common over \(\varrho \in [\rho ,\rho ^2]\).

Define

\begin{align*} B_i&=q_{i-1}T_i\mathbf{1},& D_i&=T_i-(T_i\mathbf{1})\otimes q_i,\\ G_i&=\{|B_i-1|\le 1/2\},& M_i&=\mathbf{1}_{G_i}/B_i,\\ S_i&=M_iT_i+\mathbf{1}_{G_i^c}(\mathbf{1}\otimes q_i),& a_i&=M_i(T_i\mathbf{1}-B_i\mathbf{1}),\\ b_i&=M_iq_{i-1}D_i,& L_i&=M_iD_i. \end{align*}

The positive reset kernel \(S_i\) need not be Markov, but \(q_{i-1}S_i\mathbf{1}=1\). Its bad-block branch preserves incoming mass and restores the heat shape. Let \(\mathscr F_s=\sigma (\omega _{u,z}:1\le u\le s)\) and \(\mathcal B_i=\mathscr F_{N_i}\). Each block is independent of \(\mathcal B_{i-1}\). For each noninitial block \(i\ge 2\) with \(j_i\ge J_0\) and \(N\ge N_*\), and every \(\mathcal B_{i-1}\)-measurable zero-mass accessible row \(\eta \), Lemma A.1 yields, after choosing \(J_0\) large enough,

\begin{align} \mathbb{E} (\|\eta L_i\|^2\mid \mathcal B_{i-1})&\le \kappa \|\eta \|^2, &\kappa &<1,\tag{A.15}\\ \mathbb{E} (\|b_i\|^2\mid \mathcal B_{i-1})&\le C/j_i, &\mathbb{E} (|\eta a_i|^2\mid \mathcal B_{i-1})&\le (C/j_i)\|\eta \|^2, \nonumber \\ |\eta \mathbb{E} (a_i\mid \mathcal B_{i-1})|&\le (C/j_i)\|\eta \|. &&\nonumber \end{align}

Norms in an input and output carry their respective ages. The first coefficient is at most \(4(a/b+C/j_i)\), which is below one since \(12/\rho <1\). For the drift, use \(\mathbb{E} (\eta T_i\mathbf{1}\mid \mathcal B_{i-1})=0\), \(|M_i-1|\le 2|B_i-1|\), and conditional Cauchy–Schwarz. No samplewise operator bound is asserted.

Lemma A.2 (Fourth moments, including microscopic blocks). There are common \(C,N_*,J_0\) such that

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} |B_i-1|^4\le C/(j_i+1)^2, \qquad N\ge N_*,\quad 1\le i\le K-J_0.\tag{A.16}\end{equation}

Proof. For \(a>0\), Lemma A.1 first gives \(\operatorname {Var}B_i\le C_v/j_i\), independently of fourth moments. Choose \(J_v\ge \max (J_{\rm raw},C_v,1)\), so the variance ceiling is one. The heat row satisfies

\begin{equation*} D(q_a)=a,\qquad \|q_a\|_2^2=q_{2a}(0)\le C/a,\qquad a/h\le 3/(\rho -3), \end{equation*}
and, for every fixed \(\widehat t>0\),
\begin{equation*} \sum _zq_a(z)e^{2\widehat t|z|/\sqrt h} \le 4e^{6\widehat t^2/(\rho -3)}. \end{equation*}
The last bound follows by four signed exponential projections and \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\). These verify the spread, \(\ell ^2\), exponential localization and variance hypotheses of [3, Theorem 1.11, (1.28)–(1.33)]. Obtain its duration threshold \(L_4\) and constant \(C_4\) with these fixed data; then set \(h_*=\lceil \max (1,L_4,2\rho ^2)\rceil \). The external theorem gives (A.16) on every noninitial block with \(h>h_*\).

For \(h\le h_*\), including the initial point-start block, use a direct Gaussian calculation. The partition mass is a mixture of path weights under a deterministic probability path law. A fixed path has weight \(Y=e^{\sqrt v G-v/2}\), \(v=h\beta _N^2\), because physical times correspond to independent noise coordinates even at spatial revisits. Jensen gives

\begin{align*} \mathbb{E} |B-1|^4 &\le \mathbb{E} |Y-1|^4=e^{6v}-4e^{3v}+6e^v-3\\ &\le 21e^{6v}v^2 \le 21h_*^2e^{6h_*}\nu _N^2\qquad (\nu _N\le 1). \end{align*}

For the middle inequality the expression and its first derivative vanish at zero and its second derivative is at most \(42e^{6v}\). We used \(\beta _N^2=\log (1+\nu _N)\le \nu _N\). Since \(\nu _NK\le C_{I,\rho }\) and \(j_i+1\le K\), this proves the required bound. The first block has \(N_1\le 2\rho ^2\le h_*\). Finally choose \(J_0\ge \max (J_v,2)\) for contraction and all later absorptions, and enlarge \(N_*\). Thus the fourth-moment constants are chosen before the final contraction threshold. The external theorem is never applied at zero spread or below its minimum duration. □

Lemma A.3 (A finite reset history). For \(N\ge N_*\) and \(1\le r\le t\le K-J_0\), start \(x_{r-1}=q_{r-1}\) and evolve \(x_i=x_{i-1}S_i=R_iq_i+v_i\), where \(v_i\mathbf{1}=0\). With \(J=K-t\),

\begin{equation} \max _{r-1\le i\le t}\mathbb{E} R_i^2\le 2,\qquad \mathbb{E} \|v_i\|^2\le C/j_i,\qquad \mathbb{E} \max _{r\le i\le t}|R_i-1|^2\le C/J.\tag{A.17}\end{equation}
The constants are common over all admissible \(N,r,t,\varrho \) and compact critical parameter interval \(I\).

Proof. The exact update is

\begin{equation*} R_i=R_{i-1}+v_{i-1}a_i,\qquad v_i=R_{i-1}b_i+v_{i-1}L_i. \end{equation*}
In particular, the shape update uses the old mass. For \(U_i=\mathbb{E} R_i^2\), \(V_i=\mathbb{E} \|v_i\|^2\), choose a fixed \(\gamma \in (\kappa ,1)\). Conditional estimates (A.15) and Young’s inequality give
\begin{align*} V_i&\le \gamma V_{i-1}+CU_{i-1}/j_i,\\ U_i&\le U_{i-1}+Cj_i^{-1}\sqrt {U_{i-1}V_{i-1}} +Cj_i^{-1}V_{i-1}. \end{align*}

All moments are finite by finite-step induction. Put \(H=\max U_i\). Since \(j_{i-1}=j_i+1\), iteration gives \(V_i\le CH/j_i\). Summing the second inequality gives \(H\le 1+CH/\sqrt J\); increase \(J_0\) to obtain \(H\le 2\). Decompose the scalar increments into their \(\mathcal B_{i-1}\)-conditional drift and martingale difference. The drift has \(L^2\) norm at most \(Cj_i^{-3/2}\), and the martingale increment has squared \(L^2\) norm at most \(Cj_i^{-2}\). Minkowski’s inequality and Doob’s inequality give the last assertion.

When \(r=1\), the first block is handled separately. Two walks have at most \(N_1\) common physical times, so

\begin{equation*} \mathbb{E} \sum _z\frac {(Z_{0,N_1}(0,z)-q_{N_1}(z))^2}{q_{N_1}(z)} \le (1+\nu _N)^{N_1}-1\le C_\rho \nu _N. \end{equation*}
Indeed each pair-path weight has expected product at most \((1+\nu _N)^{N_1}\), while its first moment is the walk kernel. Here \(R_1=1\) exactly and \(\mathbb{E} \|v_1\|^2\le 4C_\rho \nu _N\le C/j_1\). The resulting geometric initial term is bounded by \(C/j_i\) at subsequent blocks, so the preceding argument applies. Lemma A.1 is not used at age zero. □

For the physical row

\begin{equation*} y_t=\frac {q_{r-1}T_r\cdots T_t}{\prod _{i=r}^t B_i},\qquad R_t^{\rm ph}=y_t\mathbf{1},\qquad \mu _t=y_t/R_t^{\rm ph},\qquad \Omega =\bigcap _{i=r}^tG_i, \end{equation*}

Lemma A.2 and a union bound give \(\mathbb{P} (\Omega ^c)\le C/J\). On \(\Omega \), physical and reset rows agree. Therefore, on \(A=\Omega \cap \{|R_t^{\rm ph}-1|\le 1/2\}\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb{P} (A^c)\le C/J,\qquad \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_A\|\mu _t-q_t\|^2]\le C/J.\tag{A.18}\end{equation}

The denominator costs at most four in the squared norm. These indicators remain in every subsequent estimate.

A.3 The terminal response and a common buffer

Let \(M_N(h)=\mathbb{E} [Z_{0,h}(0,\mathbf{1})^2]\). The full-horizon renewal input from [1, (4.1), Claim B.1] gives \(\sup _{h\le N}\nu _NM_N(h)\le C\). For a deterministic accessible zero-mass row \(\eta \) at time \(n\), first-noise expansion yields

\begin{equation} \eta Z_{n,N}f=\eta q_{N-n}f+ \sum _{u=n+1}^N\sum _z(\eta q_{u-n})(z)\xi _{u,z}Z_{u,N}(z,f).\tag{A.19}\end{equation}

The terms are orthogonal: for different first-noise times integrate the earlier centered noise, and for equal times use independence at different sites. In particular

\begin{equation} H_{n,N}(\eta )^2:=\mathbb{E} |\eta Z_{n,N}\mathbf{1}|^2 =\nu _N\sum _{r=1}^{N-n}\|\eta q_r\|_2^2M_N(N-n-r).\tag{A.20}\end{equation}

By (A.2) and \(\max q_{n+r}\le C/(n+r)\),

\begin{align} \sum _{r\ge 1}\|\eta q_r\|_2^2 &\le C\sum _{k+l\ge 1}\frac {|c_{kl}^{(n)}(\eta )|^2}{k+l} \le C_\alpha \|\eta \|_{n,-\alpha }^2.\tag{A.21}\end{align}

For degree \(d\ge 1\), the summation used here is \(\sum _{r\ge 1}(n/(n+r))^d/(n+r)\le 1/d\), by comparison with its decreasing integral. Hence \(H_{n,N}(\eta )\le C\|\eta \|_{n,-\alpha }\), including when the terminal slab is not subcritical.

For deterministic bounded \(f\), positivity gives \(|Z_{u,N}(z,f)|\le \|f\|_\infty Z_{u,N}(z,\mathbf{1})\). The deterministic term in (A.19), for \(n\le N/2\), is at most \(C_\alpha \|f\|_\infty \sqrt {n/N}\|\eta \|_{n,-\alpha }\): use the heat-relative \(L^2\) norm and \((n/N)^d\le C_\alpha (n/N)(1+d)^{-\alpha }\). Thus

\begin{equation} \big (\mathbb{E} |\eta Z_{n,N}f|^2\big )^{1/2} \le C\|f\|_\infty \|\eta \|_{n,-\alpha }.\tag{A.22}\end{equation}

The same estimate holds conditionally on \(\mathscr F_n\) for a past-measurable accessible row with finite second norm moment. Its row is held fixed and only the independent future noise is averaged.

Assume \(p=K-2J\), \(t=K-J\), \(J\ge J_0\), \(2J\le K-1\), and put \(m=N_p\), \(n=N_t\le N/2\). A signed reset history on these \(J\) common blocks starting from a past-measurable zero-mass row \(w\) has state \(r_\ell q+u_\ell \), initially \((r_0,u_0)=(0,w)\). Put \(W=\mathbb{E} \|w\|^2\), \(U_\ell =\mathbb{E} r_\ell ^2\) and \(V_\ell =\mathbb{E} \|u_\ell \|^2\). All indices on this buffer are between \(J\) and \(2J-1\). From (A.15),

\begin{equation*} V_\ell \le \gamma V_{\ell -1}+CU_{\ell -1}/J. \end{equation*}

For \(H=\mathbb{E} \max _{\ell \le J}|r_\ell |^2\), iteration gives \(V_\ell \le \gamma ^\ell W+CH/J\). The drift and martingale estimates then give

\begin{align*} \|\max |\text {drift}|\|_2&\le C\sqrt W/J+C\sqrt {H/J},\\ \|\max |\text {martingale}|\|_2&\le C\sqrt {W/J}+C\sqrt {H/J}. \end{align*}

Absorb the term \(CH/J\) in \(H\le CW/J+CH/J\), increasing \(J_0\) once. It follows that

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} \max _{\ell \le J}|r_\ell |^2\le CW/J,\qquad \mathbb{E} \|u_\ell \|^2\le \gamma ^\ell W+CW/J^2.\tag{A.23}\end{equation}

Run the full reset history to \(m\) and write \(x_m=R_mq_m+v_m\). Through the same subsequent kernels run histories starting at \(q_m\) and \(v_m\). Denote their terminal states by \(y_n=R_yq_n+v_y\) and \(z_n=rq_n+u\). Linearity gives \(x_n=R_my_n+z_n\). With \(R_x=x_n\mathbf{1}\), the exact cancellation is

\begin{equation} \frac {x_n}{R_x}-\frac {y_n}{R_y} =\frac {u}{R_x}-\frac {r v_y}{R_xR_y}.\tag{A.24}\end{equation}

In particular no division by \(R_m\) occurs. By Lemma A.3, \(W=\mathbb{E} \|v_m\|^2\le C/J\) and \(\mathbb{E} \|v_y\|^2\le C/J\). Thus (A.23) gives

\begin{equation*} \|r\|_2\le C/J,\qquad (\mathbb{E} \|u\|^2)^{1/2}\le C(\gamma ^{J/2}/\sqrt J+J^{-3/2})\le CJ^{-3/2}. \end{equation*}

Let

\begin{equation} \mathcal G=\bigcap _{i=1}^tG_i \cap \{|R_x-1|\le 1/2\}\cap \{|R_y-1|\le 1/2\}\in \mathscr F_n.\tag{A.25}\end{equation}

The preceding fourth-moment and reset estimates show \(\mathbb{P} (\mathcal G^c)\le C/J\). On \(\mathcal G\), the normalized reset histories equal the physical rows

\begin{equation*} \mu _n=\frac {Z_{0,n}(0,\cdot )}{Z_{0,n}(0,\mathbf{1})},\qquad \widehat \mu _{m,n}= \frac {q_mZ_{m,n}}{q_mZ_{m,n}\mathbf{1}}. \end{equation*}

Their difference \(\eta \) has zero mass. From (A.24) and Cauchy–Schwarz,

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}\|\eta \|_{n,-\alpha }]\le CJ^{-3/2}.\tag{A.26}\end{equation}

Only a first norm moment is claimed: the product \(r v_y\) need not have the additional moments needed to square this bound. Conditioning in (A.22) gives, for deterministic bounded \(f\),

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}|\mu _nZ_{n,N}f- \widehat \mu _{m,n}Z_{n,N}f|] \le C\|f\|_\infty J^{-3/2}.\tag{A.27}\end{equation}

Each physical history separately satisfies \(\mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}\|\mu -q_n\|_{n,-\alpha }^2]\le C/J\) by (A.18). First collision and the full critical renewal bound give

\begin{equation*} \mathbb{E} (q_nZ_{n,N}\mathbf{1})^2 =1+\sum _{u=n+1}^Nq_{2u}(0)\nu _NM_N(N-u) \le 1+C\log (N/n)\le CJ. \end{equation*}

Apply (A.22) to the difference of each row from \(q_n\), conditionally on \(\mathscr F_n\). For \(X=\mu _nZ_{n,N}\mathbf{1}\) and \(Y=\widehat \mu _{m,n}Z_{n,N}\mathbf{1}\) this proves

\begin{equation} \mathbb{E} [\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal G}(X^2+Y^2)]\le CJ.\tag{A.28}\end{equation}

This is an integrated estimate on a past-measurable event. It is not a pointwise bound on the conditional second moment of every possible past. Every threshold and constant in this appendix is uniform over the compact ratio interval, so these conclusions may be used with the growing cutoff families specified in the main text.
